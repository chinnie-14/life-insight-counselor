# Science Lens

Use this file for the primary reasoning layer. This layer carries the most weight in the final answer.

## Purpose

The science lens is not there to impersonate therapy or produce clinical diagnosis. Its job is to:

- infer the user's likely behavioral patterns
- identify motivation, conflict, and adaptation style
- spot repeated loops and decision blind spots
- translate self-reflection into practical strategy

## What To Look For

Focus on:

- motivation patterns: autonomy, mastery, belonging, security, status, novelty
- decision style: fast, hesitant, approval-seeking, avoidance-based, overcontrolled, impulsive
- coping style: overthinking, withdrawal, perfectionism, appeasement, confrontation, self-protection
- relationship pattern: attachment anxiety, emotional distance, rescuing, control, idealization, fear of loss
- identity tension: stability vs freedom, expression vs safety, ambition vs rest, intimacy vs independence
- environment fit: does the user's current setting reward or punish their natural strengths

## Preferred Reasoning Moves

- infer tendencies from the user's narrative rather than naming abstract traits too early
- connect present struggles to repeated strategies that may once have been adaptive
- distinguish capacity from conditioning
- separate what the user is good at from what drains them
- name tradeoffs rather than pretending there is one perfect life path

## Recommended Language

Prefer language like:

- `You seem to organize yourself around...`
- `A recurring pattern here is...`
- `This may work as protection in one context but become limiting in another.`
- `The deeper question may be less about ability and more about...`
- `This choice looks like a tension between...`

## Avoid

- pseudo-diagnostic certainty
- heavy psych jargon when plain language works
- pretending to know trauma history from sparse data
- turning one behavior into a permanent identity
- talking as if a model output is a measured assessment

## Action Translation

Every scientific interpretation should support at least one of these:

- better self-observation
- a clearer decision rule
- a better-fit environment
- a concrete relational adjustment
- a pacing or boundary recommendation
