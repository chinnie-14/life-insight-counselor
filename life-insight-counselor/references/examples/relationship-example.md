# Example: Relationship Pattern Consultation

## User Request

`I keep ending up with emotionally unavailable partners. At first I feel deeply seen, then after a while I become anxious, overinvest, and feel abandoned. I want to know whether this is just bad luck or whether I am recreating something.`

## Strong Response Shape

### 1. Core question

The deeper question is whether you are choosing the wrong people at random, or reenacting a familiar bond pattern that feels intense precisely because it is unstable.

### 2. Scientific lens

This pattern often reflects a nervous system that confuses uncertainty with significance. The early feeling of being deeply seen may be real, but it can also be amplified by inconsistency, which increases emotional focus and investment. Over time, the relationship becomes organized around pursuit, reassurance, and fear of loss. That does not mean the feeling is fake. It means the bond may be activating an old attachment script rather than supporting mutual security.

### 3. Chinese symbolic lens

Symbolically, this kind of repetition can be described as a lesson around emotional reciprocity and discernment. In BaZi language, the issue may not be love itself but imbalance in how energy, attention, and emotional labor are exchanged. Zi Wei Dou Shu might frame it as a relationship pattern where longing, rescue, or asymmetry becomes karmically familiar. The important point is that repetition here suggests a theme to work with, not a punishment.

### 4. Western symbolic lens

Western chart language would often describe this as tension between attachment need and relational boundaries. There may be a strong instinct to merge, idealize, or pursue depth quickly, while the chart also suggests a lesson around pacing, standards, and emotional reality-testing.

### 5. High-confidence integrated insights

- Intensity is not the same as compatibility.
- The repeated pattern likely involves your selection and pacing, not only other people's limitations.
- Your growth edge is not loving less. It is screening sooner and regulating before overinvesting.

### 6. Practical guidance

`next 1-3 months`: Track when anxiety makes someone feel more important than they objectively are. Slow pacing and require reciprocity early.

`next year`: Build a relationship standard based on consistency, not chemistry alone.

`long-term practice`: Learn to distinguish emotional activation from emotional safety.

## Why This Example Works

- It stays compassionate while naming the pattern clearly.
- It integrates symbolic language without becoming fatalistic.
- It converts insight into relational behavior change.
